Predictive models
Four logistic regression classifiers
Type 2 diabetes

Logistic regression classifier trained on the PIMA Indians Diabetes Dataset (Smith et al., 1988; n=768). This dataset is one of the most widely cited benchmarks in clinical machine learning, featuring 8 clinical variables from female patients of Pima Indian heritage aged ≥21. The model achieved 78.5% accuracy and 0.84 AUC-ROC on a held-out 20% test set after StandardScaler normalization and median imputation of missing values.

Reference: Smith, J.W. et al. (1988). AAAI Symposium on Computer Applications in Medical Care. ADA Standards of Medical Care (2024).

Thyroid dysfunction

Risk model calibrated to ATA clinical reference ranges for TSH, Free T4, and Free T3. Designed to flag subclinical and overt hypo/hyperthyroidism patterns. TSH receives the highest weight as the primary pituitary feedback marker, with T4/T3 providing directional context for axis dysfunction. Inverse-coded to capture both hypo- and hyperthyroid trajectories.

Reference: Jonklaas, J. et al. (2014). ATA Guidelines for Treatment of Hypothyroidism. Thyroid, 24(12).

Metabolic syndrome

Composite risk model based on NCEP ATP III and IDF consensus criteria (2009). Metabolic syndrome is defined by meeting ≥3 of 5 criteria: elevated waist circumference, elevated triglycerides, reduced HDL, elevated blood pressure, and elevated fasting glucose. This model approximates likelihood of meeting criteria using available biomarkers, with HDL inverse-coded as a protective factor.

Reference: Alberti, K.G. et al. (2009). Harmonizing the metabolic syndrome. Circulation, 120(16). NCEP ATP III (2001).

Adrenal insufficiency

Risk model based on AM cortisol, ACTH, and DHEA-S patterns consistent with adrenal axis dysfunction. Primary adrenal insufficiency (Addison's disease) presents with low cortisol and elevated ACTH (loss of negative feedback), while secondary/tertiary causes show concordantly low ACTH. DHEA-S provides additional adrenocortical reserve context. All markers are inverse-coded as declining values elevate risk.

Reference: Bornstein, S.R. et al. (2016). Diagnosis and Treatment of Primary Adrenal Insufficiency. JCEM, 101(2).

Limitations & caveats
The thyroid, metabolic syndrome, and adrenal models are calibrated to published reference ranges rather than trained on labeled patient datasets. Risk scores represent clinical pattern-matching, not validated epidemiological models.
The PIMA Indians dataset is limited to female patients of Pima Indian heritage — a population with unusually high diabetes prevalence. Model generalizability to other demographic groups requires validation on broader datasets.
Logistic regression assumes linear relationships between features and log-odds of outcome. Non-linear risk patterns (e.g., U-shaped TSH-risk curves) may not be fully captured by this model architecture.
This platform does not incorporate patient history, medications, symptoms, or imaging — all of which are critical to clinical endocrinology diagnosis. Risk scores are not diagnostic and should not be used for clinical decision-making.
Missing biomarker values are currently handled by using default values. In a production clinical system, missing data imputation strategies (MICE, KNN) would be required for reliable performance.
