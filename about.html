Predictive Models
Four logistic regression classifiers
Type 2 Diabetes

Logistic regression classifier trained on the PIMA Indians Diabetes Dataset (Smith et al., 1988; n=768) — one of the most widely cited benchmarks in clinical ML. Features: plasma glucose, BMI, HbA1c, fasting insulin, age, pregnancies, skin thickness, diabetes pedigree function. Achieved 78.5% accuracy and 0.84 AUC-ROC on held-out 20% test set after StandardScaler normalization and median imputation.

Reference: Smith, J.W. et al. (1988). AAAI Symposium. ADA Standards of Medical Care (2024).

Thyroid Dysfunction

Risk model calibrated to ATA clinical reference ranges for TSH (0.4–4.0 mIU/L), Free T4 (0.8–1.8 ng/dL), and Free T3 (2.3–4.2 pg/mL). TSH receives highest weight as the primary pituitary feedback marker. T4 and T3 are inverse-coded to capture both hypo- and hyperthyroid trajectories simultaneously.

Reference: Jonklaas, J. et al. (2014). ATA Guidelines. Thyroid, 24(12), 1670–1751.

Metabolic Syndrome

Composite risk model based on NCEP ATP III and IDF consensus (2009). Metabolic syndrome: ≥3 of 5 criteria — elevated waist circumference, elevated triglycerides (>150 mg/dL), reduced HDL (<40 M / <50 F), elevated blood pressure, elevated fasting glucose. HDL inverse-coded as protective factor.

Reference: Alberti, K.G. et al. (2009). Harmonizing Metabolic Syndrome. Circulation, 120(16).

Adrenal Insufficiency

Risk model based on AM cortisol, ACTH, and DHEA-S patterns. Primary adrenal insufficiency: low cortisol + elevated ACTH (loss of negative feedback). Secondary/tertiary: concordantly low ACTH. DHEA-S provides adrenocortical reserve context. All markers inverse-coded — declining values elevate risk score.

Reference: Bornstein, S.R. et al. (2016). Diagnosis of Primary Adrenal Insufficiency. JCEM, 101(2), 364–389.

Limitations & caveats
Thyroid, metabolic syndrome, and adrenal models are calibrated to published reference ranges rather than trained on labeled patient datasets. Risk scores represent evidence-based pattern-matching, not validated epidemiological models.
The PIMA Indians dataset is limited to female patients of Pima Indian heritage — a population with unusually high diabetes prevalence (~34.9%). Generalizability to other demographics requires validation on broader, more diverse clinical datasets.
Logistic regression assumes linear relationships between features and log-odds of outcome. Non-linear risk patterns (e.g., U-shaped TSH-risk curves in subclinical thyroid disease) may not be fully captured by this model architecture.
The platform does not incorporate patient history, medications, symptoms beyond the provided checklist, or imaging — all critical to clinical endocrinology. Risk scores are not diagnostic and must not be used for clinical decision-making.
Symptom weights are clinician-estimated rather than derived from published symptom-prevalence data. A future iteration should validate symptom contributions against prospective cohort data.
